**Crispin Rendon** (1950–2025) was a prominent genealogist and researcher widely recognized for his extensive work on Northeastern Mexican genealogy. A longtime resident of Riverside, California, he was beloved in the Hispanic genealogy community for compiling genealogical reports and sharing his findings completely free of charge.

Born in Texas as one of 14 children, Crispin served as a medic in the U.S. Army before earning a degree in Biological Conservation from California State University. He worked as an agricultural biologist for the California Department of Food and Agriculture prior to his retirement. He passed away in September 2025 at the age of 74.

Beginning his research in 1990, Crispin spent decades transcribing Catholic church and civil records from the Mexican states of **Nuevo León, Coahuila, and Tamaulipas** where his ancestors were from. He built a personal genealogical database of over 600,000 individuals.

He was a frequent attendee and speaker at the annual Texas Hispanic Genealogical and Historical Conference, hosted by the various Hispanic genealogical societies in Texas. He compiled several multi-volume PDF books listing early families for specific towns. These are based on information obtained from church marriage records with additional data from some baptism and death records.

Alongside traditional paper trails, he pioneered efforts to organize Y-DNA and mtDNA test results for lineages originating from Northeastern Mexico. He even helped personally fund dozens of DNA tests for specific descendants of known lineages.
