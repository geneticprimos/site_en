A little history on this site and its precursors. My name is David and my genealogy journey started in earnest in 2015. As I got more involved in DNA testing and reaching out to people who shared common ancestors from Northeastern Mexico, I realized there was no active group of people working together on pairing Y-DNA and mtDNA to lineages of that region of interest. There were definitely individuals working on their own lines but not at a scale I was hoping to find.

The closest I found was some work by the genealogist, [Crispin Rendon](about-crispin-rendon). In March 2016, I emailed him to ask about the source of the Y-DNA data from his 2009 Garza ancestor report. He emailed me the updated version of the report. He also added me to his emailing list in which he would share his various genealogical reports and let everyone know about any upcoming conferences.

In those days, he used a series of EarthLink webspaces to share his reports. He even partnered with some of the Hispanic genealogical societies from Texas to host some of the reports on their websites as well. His thinking was that it would help give them more exposure. Each of the webspaces was limited to 10 MB of space and about 1 GB of bandwidth per month. As he got more popular, those webspaces would reach their limit and would become unavailable for the rest of the month.

These were the URLs of the EarthLink webspaces he used back then, they no longer work but you can find mentions of some online. Some have archived copies of how they used to look but their links do not work. The last 2 don't have archived copies.

When he told me of the traffic limit he had, I suggested using DropBox or Google Drive. After a few attempts at convincing him that a change would be for the best, I realized the only way he would actually change is if I just made it myself.

In January 2018, I placed all the reports of his that I could find into a DropBox folder. Unfortunately by then DropBox had made a change that wouldn't allow me to host a simple web page with the links to his files in the same way his webspaces worked. I found an alternative hosting site called UpDog that would host a site like DropBox used to allow. Again this URL no longer works but this is an archived copy of how it used to look. Nothing fancy.

[https://dna.updog.co/CrispinRendon/](https://web.archive.org/web/20180115151206/https://dna.updog.co/CrispinRendon/)

Crispin liked it and switched over. He would send me new files to upload every now and then after this change. He cancelled his old webspaces that he was paying \$5 per month just to share something freely. While this setup I had made for him was completely free.

In June 2019, I had a conversation about an idea for a future website with a fellow genealogist, Martin of [Genealogia Norestense](https://genealogianorestense.com). My goal was to better share our genetic genealogy research findings. He came up with the Genetic Primos name. Later in September the topic came up again and he pushed me to register the domain and suggested setting up a WordPress site like he had done. His thinking was that one day when we aren't around the domain would eventually expire but the free WordPress site would still exist. So I did it.

The owner of Updog decided to shut down the hosting services due to the lack of resources to combat users from exploiting his services for the purpose of creating spam sites. Which meant the Updog link Crispin was sharing stopped working in February 2020. That same month I setup the next iteration of Crispin's reports page to the following link. The first URL would redirect to the second. Both go to the same archived copy.

[http://geneticprimos.com/crispin-rendon](https://web.archive.org/web/20251003140834/https://geneticprimos.wordpress.com/crispin-rendon/)
[https://geneticprimos.wordpress.com/crispin-rendon/](https://web.archive.org/web/20251003140834/https://geneticprimos.wordpress.com/crispin-rendon/)

I later had to switch storing the reports from DropBox to Google Drive due to issues with viewing files bigger than 1 MB on some web browsers. Interestingly enough, EarthLink retired their webspaces in July 2020.

I was never satisfied with the limited options I had for the WordPress site and I didn't do much on it. In June 2024 I placed the DNA related genealogical reports into a spreadsheet for ease of use. This made it unnecessary to make further edits on WordPress page. Moving forward, all updates were to these reports that would only require modifying the spreadsheet.

After the passing of Crispin in 2025, I considered working on the site once again. In early 2026, I began on this latest iteration. It was more technical with a steeper learning curve but gave me the options I was looking for to craft the site more like I wanted.

This iteration uses Obsidian to create text files, Quartz to convert some of those files into webpages, GitHub to host the webpages, and Netlify to deploy the site.
